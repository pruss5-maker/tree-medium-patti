# Teleprompter — the voice first, then the looking

**Reader:** Patti, to camera  
**Length:** about 2 minutes 50 seconds  
**Pace:** lived first, then the looking. Pause at each `/`.  
**Spoken words:** ~440

Order: the trees name KELA and ask about protection. Then she asks about the Hebrew word. That looking opens David and Goliath — a story any Christian would swear they knew. Then where it actually lives, then the deeper meaning.

The looking included asking AI. The script says “I asked about the Hebrew word” so the camera stays on the trees and the text, not on a product.

---

The trees spoke first. /

I was walking into my apartment. Trees all around me. They asked me to call their collective voice KELA. /

The name came before any book. /

Then they asked me something I have never forgotten. /

Why haven’t you asked the trees to protect your mind and energy yet? /

That is how this started. Not in a church. Not in a library. A collective voice, and a name. /

After that, I went looking. I asked about the Hebrew word KELA. What it actually meant. I used AI to help me sit with the word. People fear AI. I get that. For me it has been a way to uncover patterns that need to be seen. Not a god. A lens. The trees named it. I asked. The patterns were already there. /

If you grew up in church, you would say with certainty you know David and Goliath. Little guy. Giant. Slingshot. Stone to the head. God helps the underdog. /

I would have said that too. An American. An ex-Christian. I did not even know the word Tanakh. /

Did we really know the true meaning? /

No. /

Most of the scripture Christians read has already been translated. We were not holding the first tongue. We were holding English. /

And even the Hebrew of the Tanakh is a writing-down. Before it was a page, it was a story people told. Some of the names in it are not even Hebrew. So the story was carried through more than one language before it ever reached a church pew. /

Of course we thought we knew it. We knew the last telling. /

Here is where that chapter actually sits. /

What a lot of churches call the Old Testament is the Hebrew library of scripture. Jewish people call that whole shelf the Tanakh. Same books. Different name. /

Inside that shelf is a book English Bibles call First Samuel. In Hebrew the first half is Shmuel Aleph. Chapter seventeen is David and Goliath. /

That is all “chapter 17” means. One chapter in that old Hebrew book. The Sunday-school story. The last telling most of us ever got. /

I sat with what that word was holding, and I was amazed. /

Qela is the sling in David’s hand. Not a toy slingshot. A shepherd’s throw. Leather. Cords. One stone. /

The same word is also the cloth hangings of a holy courtyard. Fabric stretched to make a sacred space. A woven wall. Many threads, one shelter. /

The valley is named for a tree. /

The giant’s spear is compared to a weaver’s beam. That is the thick wooden bar on a loom, the thing cloth hangs from. A tree turned into weight. /

And the line that stopped me: he was stronger with the qela and the stone. And a sword is not in his hand. /

English kept the fight. It dropped the grove. It dropped the hanging. It dropped the no-sword. /

So we got a sports story. Be brave. Beat the bigger thing. Stay on a battlefield. /

What if that is the same kind of mess as our minds? /

A living word with many rooms, reduced to one loud thought. The story itself got translated like a mind gets crowded. The Goliath on the page became the Goliath in the field. /

I can’t help how I feel it should have been told. /

Not a little guy getting lucky. /

A loud thought standing in the gap of the mind. The Goliath mess. Repeating. Unkind. Taking the room where peace wants to live. /

And not only the mind. /

If you have ever felt rage when you are disconnected, I want to say this gently. We have been subject to more than a Goliath of the mind. We have been subject to a Goliath of the body. /

The fight-story does not just live in words. It lives in the shoulders. In the jaw. In the armor we were taught to wear. Champion. Beat the bigger thing. Do not feel the grove. /

When you cannot feel the courtyard, the body still wants the field cleared. That can come through as rage. Not because you are broken. Because something in you remembers sanctuary, and the occupation is in the flesh too. /

And the answer is not more armor. A grove around you. A fabric of trees. One clear stone. No sword. Mind and body. /

That is KELA. /

Then I sat back and looked at the name the trees asked me to call them. /

And a bigger picture came. /

Not just a sling in one old chapter. A way of seeing. How a living word gets reduced to one loud fight. How a mind gets crowded the same way. How a grove can be a courtyard around you. The name itself was the wider lens. /

Pulling back, I could see the story had been written and rewritten to fit a masculine narrative again. Champion. Armor. Beat the bigger man. /

A courtyard of cloth and trees does not serve that story. So the weave got dropped. The hanging got dropped. The no-sword got dropped. What survived is a fight between men. /

The collective voice called it. I asked what the word meant. A story any Christian could swear they knew opened. Then sitting with the name showed me it was never only a boy and a giant. /

I can’t help feeling this is the story. The intention to ask for protection from the Goliath mess of our minds. /

What has been standing in plain sight has always been the trees. /

Not hidden. Not far. The grove around the apartment. The valley named for a tree. The wood in the spear. The hanging that makes a sanctuary. Right there, while we told a fight. /

I feel the goddess of creation sitting in excitement. Waiting. For one person to dig deeper. I asked. I looked. I sat back with the name. /

She is not done waiting. The trees are still in plain sight. /

I am not rewriting the Bible. I am sitting beside it, amazed, because what the trees named in me came first, and the old Hebrew held it. /

If your mind has felt like a giant you cannot beat, you do not have to become a bigger giant. /

Ask the trees to hold your field. /

Your mind should be a sanctuary. Not a battlefield. /
